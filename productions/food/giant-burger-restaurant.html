🍔 Food & Activity

Food: World's Biggest Burger

Activity: Building & Operating a Giant Burger Restaurant

Concept: Tiny workers first construct a miniature burger restaurant — complete with a kitchen, grill, prep station, dining counter, and a giant neon "OPEN" sign — then cook and assemble the world's biggest burger inside it, from grinding the meat to stacking the towering layers. The entire operation is a massive adventure where every kitchen task becomes an industrial-scale challenge.

👷 Workforce & Machinery

Workers (12): 3 construction workers building the restaurant structure — walls, counter, and signage, 2 grill cooks operating a giant flat-top grill searing massive beef patties, 2 prep workers slicing giant tomato rounds and lettuce leaves, 1 baker pulling a giant sesame seed bun from a miniature brick oven, 1 sauce specialist mixing special sauce in a copper vat, 1 assembly coordinator stacking the burger layers on a steel platform, 1 expeditor arranging the final presentation, 1 cashier installing the neon OPEN sign.

Machinery: Miniature brick oven, flat-top grill with gas burners, meat grinder, industrial lettuce slicer, tomato slicing platform, sauce mixing vat with stirrer, assembly platform with a rotating turntable, tiny neon sign transformer.

📖 Story & Challenge

The giant beef patty is so heavy that when the grill cooks shrink it unevenly, one side becomes thicker than the other — threatening to make the entire towering burger lean and topple. The assembly coordinator must re-engineer the stacking order and use structural sauce placement to balance the uneven patty. Meanwhile, the neon OPEN sign short-circuits just minutes before the restaurant's grand opening.

STORY AND CHALLENGE: The restaurant is two hours from grand opening. The construction crew races to finish the storefront while the kitchen team simultaneously cooks the world's biggest burger. The giant patty has just come off the grill — but it shrank unevenly, one side noticeably thicker. If the burger is stacked this way, it will lean and topple. The assembly coordinator redesigns the stack: placing the thicker side on the left, compensating with extra sauce weight on the right, and using a toothpick structural support through the center. Meanwhile, the neon OPEN sign sparks and goes dark — the cashier traces the fault to a loose transformer wire and reconnects it just as the last sesame seed is sprinkled on the towering bun crown.
